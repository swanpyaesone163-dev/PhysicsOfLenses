Understanding refraction

Refraction occurs when light passes from one medium to another and changes speed. This change causes the ray to bend. At a curved surface, the bending is described using spherical surface formulas and optical principles.

Lenses are designed to focus or spread light rays using curved surfaces. Convex lenses converge light, while concave lenses diverge it.

  • Light bends at the boundary between media
  • Curved surfaces change the direction of rays
  • Lenses form real or virtual images

Important formulas

These equations are central to solving optics problems in this chapter.

Lens Equation

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Relates focal length f, image distance v, and object distance u.

Power of a Lens

P = 1/f

Power is measured in dioptres when f is in metres.

Magnification

m = v/u

Shows how much larger or smaller the image is compared with the object.

Refraction Rule

n = sin i / sin r

Snell’s law describes how light bends as it enters a different medium.

Q&A

Need a quick answer? Explore common questions about refraction, lenses, and image formation.

What is refraction?

It is the bending of light when it passes from one medium into another.

Why do lenses form images?

Because they bend rays so they meet at a point or appear to come from one.

How do I use the lens formula?

Use 1/f = 1/v - 1/u and solve for the missing quantity.
