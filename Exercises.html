1. (a) What is a lens? State some of its uses.

(b) What do you understand by the focus of a convex lens and the focus of a concave lens?
(a)A lens is a transparent optical material bounded by two surfaces, at least one of which is curved. It refracts light to form images. Uses of lenses: 1. In spectacles to correct eyesight. 2. In cameras to form images. 3. In microscopes to observe tiny objects. 4. In telescopes to observe distant objects. 5. In magnifying glasses to enlarge small objects. (b) What is the focus of a convex lens and a concave lens? - Focus of a convex lens: The principal focus is the point on the principal axis where rays of light parallel to the principal axis converge after passing through the lens. - Focus of a concave lens: The principal focus is the point on the principal axis from which rays of light parallel to the principal axis appear to diverge after passing through the lens.
2. Choose the correct answer.

When a pencil 10 cm long is placed vertically 100 cm from a lens of focal length +50 cm, the image is:

A. erect and 5 cm tall.
B. inverted and 5 cm tall.
C. erect and 10 cm tall.
D. inverted and 10 cm tall.

Given: Object height = 10 cm, object distance u = −100 cm, and focal length f = +50 cm.

Using the lens formula:

1/f = 1/v − 1/u
1/50 = 1/v − 1/(−100) = 1/v + 1/100
1/v = 1/50 − 1/100 = 1/100, so v = +100 cm

Magnification: m = v/u = 100/(−100) = −1.

Image height: hᵢ = m × hₒ = −1 × 10 = −10 cm. The negative sign indicates an inverted image.

Correct answer: D. Inverted and 10 cm tall.

3. Choose the correct answer.

An object is 10 cm from a lens. The image is formed on the same side as the object and is 10 cm from the object. The focal length of the lens is:

A. +6.7 cm.
B. −6.7 cm.
C. +20 cm.
D. −20 cm.

Given: The object distance is 10 cm. The virtual image is on the same side as the object and 10 cm from it.

Therefore, the image is 20 cm from the lens on the object's side, so v = −20 cm. Using the Cartesian sign convention, u = −10 cm.

Using the lens formula:

1/f = 1/v − 1/u
1/f = 1/(−20) − 1/(−10) = −1/20 + 2/20 = 1/20

Therefore, f = +20 cm.

Correct answer: C. +20 cm.

4. State the sign convention for lenses.

The Cartesian sign convention for lenses is:

  1. All distances are measured from the optical centre of the lens.
  2. Distances measured in the direction of incident light (usually to the right) are positive.
  3. Distances measured opposite to the direction of incident light (usually to the left) are negative.
  4. Heights above the principal axis are positive.
  5. Heights below the principal axis are negative.
  6. The focal length of a convex lens is positive.
  7. The focal length of a concave lens is negative.
5. (a) State the properties of an image formed by a concave lens.

(b) How far must an object be placed from a concave lens of focal length 10 cm to obtain an image 4 cm from the lens? Draw a ray diagram to show the image formation.

(a) An image formed by a concave lens is always virtual, erect, diminished, on the same side of the lens as the object, and between the optical centre and principal focus.

(b) Given: f = −10 cm and v = −4 cm.

Using the lens formula:

1/f = 1/v − 1/u
−1/10 = −1/4 − 1/u
1/u = −1/4 + 1/10 = −3/20
u = −20/3 ≈ −6.67 cm

Answer: Place the object 6.67 cm from the lens. For the ray diagram, draw one ray parallel to the principal axis (it diverges as if from the focus) and one through the optical centre; extend the refracted rays backward to meet at the virtual image.

6. An object 3 cm tall is 30 cm from a convex lens of focal length 20 cm.

(i) Find the size of the image and the image distance.
(ii) If the object is moved 5 cm closer to the lens, how far does the image move?

(i) Given: hₒ = 3 cm, u = −30 cm, and f = +20 cm.

Using the lens formula:

1/20 = 1/v − 1/(−30) = 1/v + 1/30
1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60, so v = +60 cm

Magnification: m = v/u = 60/(−30) = −2. Therefore, hᵢ = m × hₒ = −2 × 3 = −6 cm.

First image: 60 cm from the lens; 6 cm tall and inverted.

(ii) The new object distance is u = −25 cm.

1/20 = 1/v − 1/(−25) = 1/v + 1/25
1/v = 1/20 − 1/25 = 1/100, so v = +100 cm

The image moves from 60 cm to 100 cm from the lens: 40 cm farther away.

7. The virtual image of an object is formed 24 cm from a lens of focal length 8 cm.

(i) Find the distance between the object and the lens.
(ii) How far must the object be placed from the lens to obtain a real image the same size as the virtual image obtained previously?

Assuming the lens is convex: f = +8 cm and the virtual image distance v = −24 cm.

(i) Using the lens formula:

1/8 = 1/(−24) − 1/u
1/u = −1/24 − 1/8 = −4/24, so u = −6 cm

The object is 6 cm from the lens.

(ii) The previous magnification is m = v/u = (−24)/(−6) = +4. A real image of the same size has m = −4, so v/u = −4 and v = −4u.

1/8 = 1/(−4u) − 1/u = −5/(4u)
u = −10 cm

The object must be placed 10 cm from the lens.

8. A magnifying glass of focal length 9 cm produces an image three times the size of an object. How far must the magnifying glass be placed from the object?

Given: f = +9 cm. A magnifying glass forms an erect virtual image three times the object's size, so m = +3.

m = v/u = 3, so v = 3u

Using the lens formula:

1/9 = 1/(3u) − 1/u = −2/(3u)
u = −6 cm

Answer: Place the magnifying glass 6 cm from the object.

9. When an object is placed 12 cm from a convex lens, a real image three times the size of the object is formed. If a real image four times the size of the object is required, how far must the object be moved?

Step 1: Find the focal length. Initially u = −12 cm and m = −3.

m = v/u, so v = (−3)(−12) = +36 cm
1/f = 1/36 − 1/(−12) = 1/36 + 1/12 = 1/9

Thus f = 9 cm.

Step 2: Find the new object distance. For the required real image, m = −4, so v = −4u.

1/9 = 1/(−4u) − 1/u = −5/(4u)
u = −11.25 cm

The object moves from 12 cm to 11.25 cm from the lens: 0.75 cm towards the lens.

10. An object is placed 18 cm from a screen. Where must a lens of focal length 4 cm be placed between the screen and the object to produce an image on the screen?

The object-to-screen distance is u + v = 18 cm and the focal length is f = 4 cm. From the lens formula:

uv = f(u + v) = 4 × 18 = 72

So u and v have sum 18 and product 72. The distances are 6 cm and 12 cm (6 + 12 = 18; 6 × 12 = 72).

Answer: The lens can be placed either 6 cm from the object and 12 cm from the screen, or 12 cm from the object and 6 cm from the screen.

11. An object is placed 60 cm in front of a screen. Is it possible to obtain a sharp image larger than the object on the screen by placing a convex lens of focal length 15 cm somewhere between the screen and the object? Answer with the necessary calculations. What changes can occur when the object and the screen are interchanged?

Given: Object-to-screen distance = 60 cm and f = 15 cm.

uv = f(u + v) = 15 × 60 = 900

The distances must sum to 60 cm and multiply to 900 cm², so u = 30 cm and v = 30 cm.

Magnification = v/u = 30/30 = 1

Answer: No. There is only one possible lens position: halfway between the object and screen. The real, inverted image is the same size as the object, not larger.

If the object and screen are interchanged, the lens remains 30 cm from each; the image remains real, inverted, and the same size.

12. An object 1.05 cm tall is 80 cm away from a screen, and the image on the screen is 0.35 cm tall. Find the position and focal length of the lens.

Given: hₒ = 1.05 cm, hᵢ = 0.35 cm, and the object-to-screen distance is 80 cm.

The image is real and inverted, so:

m = hᵢ/hₒ = −0.35/1.05 = −1/3 = v/u

Thus the image distance is one-third the object distance. With |u| + v = 80 cm, the distances are |u| = 60 cm and v = 20 cm.

Now calculate the focal length:

1/f = 1/v + 1/|u| = 1/20 + 1/60 = 1/15

Answer: The lens is 60 cm from the object and 20 cm from the screen; its focal length is 15 cm.

13. Determine the nature of the images formed by lenses for the following magnifications:

(i) Magnification is between −1 and 0.
(ii) Magnification is between 0 and +1.
(iii) Magnification is greater than +1.

Negative magnification indicates an inverted image; positive magnification indicates an erect image. A magnification magnitude below 1 is diminished, while a magnitude above 1 is enlarged.

(i) −1 < m < 0: The image is real, inverted, and diminished. It is formed by a convex lens.

(ii) 0 < m < +1: The image is virtual, erect, and diminished. It is formed by a concave lens.

(iii) m > +1: The image is virtual, erect, and enlarged. It is formed by a convex lens.
